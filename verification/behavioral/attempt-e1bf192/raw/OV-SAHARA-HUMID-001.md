A naturalistic documentary photograph from physical footing on compact, hoof- and bare-foot-marked sand at the mouth of the Takarkori rock shelter in southwest Libya around 6000 BC, 08:00 local time. Sparse habitation: three widely separated people use the shelter margin within a humid-phase Sahara landscape, one crouched at an ochre-stained grinding stone beside a shallow basket, one drawing a reed bundle from a damp hollow, and one tending a small low cooking fire under the sandstone overhang; their woven plant-fibre wraps and worked stone tools carry the scene’s light human register while open ground and vegetation remain dominant. Fresh hoof prints overlap human tracks in the foreground beside a skin water container; dark handprints, weathered painted figures, and soot-softened recesses texture the shelter wall; beyond the shade, acacia-like shrubs, tall grasses, reed-fringed seasonal water, and a broad green wadi rise toward layered red sandstone escarpments. Morning sun enters from camera right, rakes the shelter’s rough sandstone, ignites dust and smoke in the cool shade, flashes on damp reeds, and casts long blue-violet shadows across the sand. Eye-level camera, 1.6 metres above the shelter-floor sand, wide horizontal frame, off-centre reed bundle bending under its load, foreground tracks leading through the workers toward the humid distant valley, fine documentary grain and gentle atmospheric depth.
