Wide documentary photograph from human-eye height on a packed-earth lane in Uruk, southern Mesopotamia, around 3200 BC at first light: left of centre, a scribe presses a cylinder seal into a wet clay tablet on a low reed mat while a porter swings a reed basket around him, the basket’s rim cutting across the frame. Dense urban life occupies the lane from foreground to horizon: wool-clad workers carry jars and bundled reeds, children trail beside a donkey load, merchants lift woven lids, and mudbrick compounds with reed-mat doors and freshly repaired plaster continue along both edges. Fresh clay, woven reed, bitumen-darkened jar seams, seal-marked tablets, and a cone-mosaic precinct wall carry the Late Uruk material world; a shallow canal flashes through an opening beyond the lane, with palms and marsh reeds catching the distance. Low sun enters from camera right, rakes the reed impressions in plaster, ignites suspended dust and wet clay, and leaves the lane in long blue-violet shadow; broad 16:9 frame, deep atmospheric perspective, lively off-centre motion, fine documentary grain.
