Wide documentary photograph from physical footing 1.6 metres above the sandy, stone-strewn threshold of the Takarkori rock shelter, southwest Libya, around 6000 BC at 08:00. An occupant kneels beside a low grinding stone as a small morning hearth is adjusted in the shelter shade; two other figures remain widely separated along the terrace, their light work leaving the humid-phase landscape visually dominant. Weathered sandstone overhang, fresh ash, woven plant-fibre bundles, and a few simple stone tools carry the sparse site use. Beyond the threshold, open grassland, scattered shrubs and small thorn trees follow shallow runoff toward a green basin beneath layered red rock; the foreground grit, middle-distance shelter activity, and far escarpment share one damp-season morning. Low sun rakes across the sandstone and catches pale smoke, dew-darkened grass, and the edges of stone, while long cool shelter shadows fall unevenly across the terrace; off-centre framing, a near rock partially occluding the hearth, natural depth and restrained documentary realism.
