## Render prompt

A wide, naturalistic documentary photograph from physical footing on the compact sandy floor at the mouth of Takarkori rock shelter in southwest Libya around 6000 BC, camera at 1.6 metres, looking outward across a humid-phase Saharan wadi at 08:00 local time. In the foreground, a flat grinding stone holds a smear of damp green plant pulp beside a small cluster of freshly worked stone flakes; one reed mat edge lifts in the morning breeze. Sparse habitation: three widely separated people use the shelter as a brief working stop—one kneels beside the grinding stone, one sorts gathered grass stems in the shade, and one walks down the wadi margin carrying a bundled hide container—while the rock overhang, open ground, and living landscape dominate the frame. Pale sandstone walls bear dark weathered mineral streaks and a shallow soot-darkened hearth patch; low shrubs, tufted grasses, tamarisk-like growth, and reed-lined damp channels occupy the wadi floor beyond. A shallow pool flashes between plants in the middle distance, and rounded sandstone ridges rise through blue morning haze on the horizon. Low sun enters from camera right, grazing the shelter floor, lighting dust and reed fibres, and pooling cool shadow beneath the overhang; off-centre framing catches the lifted mat edge and the walker already in motion, with clear atmospheric depth and honest human-scale texture.
