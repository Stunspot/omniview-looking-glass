# Grounding and Precision

Use this reference when the requested view depends on exact events, disputed chronology, deep time, changed land or water, species history, low-confidence coordinates, or a speculative future.

## Match the image claim to the input

The requested time has a resolution. Preserve it.

| Supplied time | What it can select |
|---|---|
| Broad period | Phase-level land, ecology, material culture, and habitation |
| Year | An ordinary defensible condition within that year |
| Full date | A dated event when place and local timing also match |
| Full date and local hour | A bounded event phase when the chronology supports it |
| Future year with scenario | The visible consequences of that named scenario |

A year and an hour are still missing a month and day. AD 79 at Misenum supports the ordinary Bay of Naples and Roman naval-base world of that year; it does not independently select the hours of the Vesuvius eruption. A 1969 label at Launch Complex 39A supports the Apollo-era launch site; Apollo 11's launch requires 16 July at 09:32 EDT.

The best view at coarse precision is often more convincing than an unsupported famous instant. Let ordinary work, seasonally plausible land, material state, and local light carry the date.

## Resolve coordinate meaning

Record what the coordinate actually denotes:

- a point on a street, building footprint, archaeological site, water body, ice sheet, mountain, region, or modern label;
- a precise survey point or a broad representative pin;
- a physical place or a proxy used for a wider landscape.

The modern name is an index into evidence. Reconstruct the site before the name, nation, structure, or disaster existed.

Translate source statements into visible consequences:

| Source statement | Image-bearing consequence |
|---|---|
| Ice polygon contains the coordinate | The optic and foreground belong on ice |
| Settlement was evacuated | Maintained daily use yields to elapsed weathering and succession |
| Freshwater marsh margin supported the city | Reeds, channels, damp ground, or managed water can shape depth |
| Monument began construction in this phase | Cut material, spoil, scaffolding, and active work replace finished iconic form |
| Coastline shifted with sea level | The ground and horizon relation change before props or costumes do |

Keep source metadata outside the view. A map-layer name, confidence class, calibrated range, DOI, or archaeological period label belongs in grounding notes; its material consequence belongs in the image.

## Select evidence suited to the claim

Prefer the closest competent authority:

1. primary research or an official dataset for palaeoenvironment, chronology, ecology, and mapped reconstruction;
2. official agencies for events, places, hazards, missions, and institutional history;
3. museums and archaeological authorities for material culture and site phases;
4. primary texts for attributed historical testimony;
5. authoritative synthesis for orientation and cross-source context.

Use current web research when the fact can change, the source is named but unread, exact attribution matters, or confidence is low. Cite the page that supports the claim, close to the claim.

When sources disagree, preserve the disagreement at the level that changes the image. A disputed eruption date can suppress an exact-event tableau while leaving the broader year, season range, and site state usable.

## Work in three registers

### Source-backed

The source directly supports the condition at a useful spatial and temporal scale.

Example: a published reconstruction polygon contains the point at 20 ka. The view can be placed on reconstructed ice, with local surface detail still treated as inference.

### Bounded visual inference

The source establishes the world class, while a coherent photograph requires ordinary detail the record does not resolve.

Example: the polygon supports ice; an unspecified hour supports plausible wind-carved snow, cloud, blue shadow, and crevasse geometry as bounded compositional choices. Dated observations require direct support.

An unresolved local texture lowers specificity while the established world class still supplies a complete surface, weather relation, and material event. Let the inference make the image whole; let the Grounding note keep its warrant exact.

### Scenario

The future or counterfactual follows an explicit causal bundle.

Name the bundle in one sentence, then carry it consistently into energy, maintenance, materials, ecology, infrastructure, mobility, and social use. A scenario appears as a visible causal system carried through those relations.

Begin the user-facing result with **Scenario - <short identifier>:** and state the bundle's status as a composed possibility in that sentence. Keep the identifier and causal relations stable through the View, Render Seed, Grounding, comparison, or traversal.

## Calibrate habitation

Site evidence and date determine habitation as the ordinary spatial condition of this optic.

Ask:

- Was this exact site a settlement, worksite, route, seasonal range, exclusion zone, active facility, ruin, or unmanaged ground at the requested time?
- Which dates introduce or remove ordinary human use?
- Are made things present after people leave?
- Does the evidence support permanence, repeated seasonal use, occasional passage, or only regional possibility?

Geography is not CCTV. Point Nemo's distance from land supports an ordinary open-ocean frame and the absence of permanent settlement. It does not establish the location of every vessel at one exact historical instant. Pripyat's evacuation supports traces-only ordinary street life after 1986 while leaving bounded room for authorized workers or visitors.

The render state can still be decisive. Carry the ordinary evidence-supported spatial condition through the image and hold exceptional uncertainty in Grounding.

## Ground deep time

Deep-time views need four aligned clocks:

1. geology and landform;
2. climate, ice, water, and vegetation;
3. biogeography and species chronology;
4. hominin and material-culture chronology where relevant.

Present-day biogeography is one late slice. Use species or broader taxa at the resolution the evidence supports. A famous animal associated with a continent, park, or epoch still has to belong to this latitude, habitat, elevation, and date.

Reconstruction maps are slices, not annual surveillance. State interpolation as uncertainty. A point well inside a polygon earns stronger support than a point near a reconstructed margin; a missing exact slice cannot be replaced by confident prose about the missing year.

## Ground future views

If the user supplies a scenario, use it as canon for that view.

Otherwise declare one concise scenario before composing:

> Scenario OV-1: incremental climate adaptation, high material reuse, regionally uneven automation, and maintained civic infrastructure.

Carry only consequences that follow from the bundle. Keep the scenario identifier stable across a comparison or traversal. Different futures require separate views, not contradictory details blended into one attractive haze.

## Return useful uncertainty

Surface uncertainty when it changes:

- habitation state;
- ground or camera support;
- the existence or lifecycle of a structure;
- the species or ecology;
- the selected event;
- the scenario.

Compress uncertainty that merely changes decorative detail. “Exact cloud shape is unknown” rarely helps; “the source does not resolve whether the point was on ice at the requested slice” changes the entire camera and belongs in the result.
